Light, metal, finish

Every construct is also a 3D model. What an agent writes (named parts in metres, joints, the order they assemble in) exports as one glTF file, and a material map finishes it. These are gallery constructs, exported unedited and lit here live: from the hologram, to the plain model, to the finished one.

Why it matters
  • Real parts. Each part is its own named object, sized in metres, carrying its role and how well it is known.
  • Joints that work. The FJ62's tailgate swings about its own hinge, here, in Blender or in a game engine.
  • The build travels with it. Every vertex knows when it appears, so an engine can assemble the model or scan it in.
  • One description, every look. The hologram, the plain model and the finished one are the same file; the finish comes from a separate material map (paint, chrome, glass, wood…), never from the construct's facts.

The finishes were chosen for this page from photographs. Shapes come from Construct's own vocabulary; there are no textures here (the Blender add-on adds wood grain and brushed metal), and parts are not merged. A blockout, a prop or a teaching model, not a finished hero asset. How it works: 3D export.
